Do Off-Amazon Sellers Want Faster Shipping Speeds?

Multi-Channel Fulfillment (MCF) • Multi-method program • Strategy decision

Qualitative interviews Triangulation Roadmap strategy Exec-ready storytelling

Executive summary: Amazon leadership faced a high-stakes decision about investing in same-day and sub-same-day shipping for off-Amazon sellers. I led a multi-method research program that resolved a long-standing internal debate, redirected a $1B+ roadmap, and reframed how leadership evaluated “speed” as a merchant value proposition.

Outcome
Prevented misaligned investment; redirected roadmap toward configurable fulfillment (2026–2028+)
Core finding
Limited merchant demand for faster-than-3-day shipping; flexibility and cost matter more
Method
7,000+ feedback signals reviewed, pilot/rollout analysis, 11× 90-minute interviews
Read the case study

Executive Summary

Despite internal pressure to mirror Amazon.com’s speed leadership, early signals suggested limited merchant demand. The research program provided decision-grade evidence that faster-than-3-day speeds would not produce the expected merchant ROI and recommended investing in configurable, merchant-centric fulfillment capabilities instead.

The business question

Should Amazon invest in developing same-day or sub-same-day shipping for off-Amazon sellers (MCF) for 2026–2028 planning?

My role

  • Senior UXR — end-to-end research owner
  • Framed the problem, designed triangulation strategy, and led qualitative fieldwork
  • Owned synthesis, storytelling, and executive readouts

Research approach

Triangulation over volume. Combined internal signals with a focused qualitative deep dive to answer not just what merchants were doing, but why.

Existing data

  • 7,000+ merchant feedback echoes (AI-assisted thematic analysis)
  • 3BDS pilot (Oct 2023) and broad rollout analysis (Sept 2024)

Primary research

  • 11 in-depth interviews (90 minutes each) with SMB DTC merchants ($1M–$50M ARR)
  • Participants: Heads of Ops, Logistics, Supply Chain; ~1,000+ MCF orders/month

Key insights

  • Limited demand: Merchants reported minimal customer pressure for same-day delivery; 2–3 day options met expectations.
  • Cost constraints: Incremental fulfillment costs rarely justified without clear ROI.
  • Flexibility > speed: Merchants prioritized custom packaging, bundling, order edits, and routing flexibility.
  • Decreasing pressure: Competitive dynamics reduced urgency to compete purely on speed.

Impact on strategy

Leadership pivoted from investing in pure speed to building configurable fulfillment capabilities that balance speed, cost, and operational control. This framing informed 2026 planning and was embedded in longer-term roadmap decisions across MCF, AFN, SCOT, and NATS.

Why Were Buy with Prime Onboarding Rates So Low?

Rapid usability research • Launch-critical risk • SKU matching

Usability testing Rapid research Stakeholder streaming

Executive summary: Ahead of a Shopify app launch, onboarding completion remained low. Rapid, streamed usability sessions identified SKU-matching failures as the primary blocker. The findings unlocked immediate investment and a redesign that materially improved onboarding efficiency.

Outcome
Unlocked $1M+ investment; enabled fuzzy SKU matching redesign
Impact
35% reduction in onboarding time; SKU parity improved from 45% → 56.2% by EOY
Root cause
Rigid exact-match SKU requirements with no recovery path
Read the case study

The business problem

Buy with Prime required merchants to onboard and map SKUs between Shopify and Amazon systems. Despite strong interest, completion rates were low and the launch risk was high with timelines locked.

My role

  • Senior UXR — self-initiated, end-to-end owner
  • Recruited merchants via Sales partnerships and ran live sessions streamed to stakeholders
  • Facilitated leadership listening sessions to drive accountability and action

Research approach

Fast, high-signal qualitative testing optimized for action: live usability testing of real onboarding flows with immediate synthesis focused on failure points.

Key insight

The primary blocker was SKU matching — exact-match requirements and confusing expectations caused merchants to fail and have no recovery path.

Impact

Research led to executive approval for investment, implementation of fuzzy SKU matching within one month, and measurable onboarding improvements (listed above). The fixes also produced cross-product benefits for MCF onboarding.
